# BUDDY AI — Power & Electrical Safety Guide

**Project:** BUDDY AI — Reception Robot for APCOER Pune  
**Target Board:** ESP32-S3 N16R8 (16MB Flash, 8MB Octal PSRAM)  
**External Supply:** 5V, 2A USB Power Adapter  
**Document Revision:** 1.0 (Hardware-Ready)  

---

## 1. Power Architecture Overview

BUDDY AI contains two distinct power voltage rails:
1. **5V Rail (VBUS / VIN):** Supplied by the USB-C port or external 5V 2A power adapter.
2. **3.3V Rail (VREG):** Generated by the onboard Low-Dropout (LDO) regulator (typically ME6211 or AMS1117-3.3) on the ESP32-S3 development board.

```
       [ 5V, 2A USB Power Source ]
                   │
                   ▼
       ┌────────────────────────┐
       │ ESP32-S3 Board 5V / VIN├──────► [ MAX98357A Amplifier VIN ]
       └───────────┬────────────┘        (Draws ~300mA - 800mA dynamic peak)
                   │
         [ Onboard 3.3V LDO ]
                   │
                   ▼
       ┌────────────────────────┐
       │     3.3V Power Rail    ├──────► [ ESP32-S3 Core & Wi-Fi ] (~160mA - 350mA)
       │                        ├──────► [ ST7789 2.8" LCD Panel ] (~40mA - 90mA)
       │                        ├──────► [ INMP441 Microphone VDD] (~1.4mA)
       │                        └──────► [ Onboard WS2812 RGB LED] (~20mA)
       └────────────────────────┘
```

## 2. Component Current Budget & Power Analysis

Here is the verified current consumption analysis across operating states:

| Component | Operating Voltage | Typical Idle Current | Peak / Active Current | Power Source |
| :--- | :---: | :---: | :---: | :---: |
| **ESP32-S3 Microcontroller** | 3.3V | 70 mA | 310 mA (Wi-Fi TX burst) | ESP32 3.3V Pin |
| **ST7789 2.8" TFT LCD** | 3.3V | 35 mA | 85 mA (100% Backlight) | ESP32 3.3V Pin |
| **INMP441 I²S Microphone** | 3.3V | 1.4 mA | 2.5 mA | ESP32 3.3V Pin |
| **Onboard WS2812 RGB LED** | 3.3V | 1 mA | 25 mA (Bright White) | Internal |
| **MAX98357A I²S Amplifier** | **5.0V** | 2.4 mA | **450 mA – 750 mA** (Loud speech into 4Ω/8Ω) | **ESP32 5V / VBUS Pin** |
| **Total Combined Load** | — | **~110 mA** | **~1,170 mA (1.17 A peak)** | **5V 2A Adapter** |

### Safety Margin Assessment:
- **Maximum Expected Peak Draw:** **$\approx 1.17\text{ A}$** (during concurrent loud speech playback + Wi-Fi transmission + full backlight).
- **Available Adapter Capacity:** **$2.0\text{ A}$** ($10.0\text{ W}$).
- **Headroom:** **$>40\%$ spare capacity**, which provides an operating margin and prevents brownout resets during peak audio surges.

## 3. Critical Voltage Rules

> [!CAUTION]
> **RULE 1: INMP441 MUST NEVER BE CONNECTED TO 5V.**  
> The INMP441 MEMS transducer and internal sigma-delta converter are rated for $1.62\text{V}$ to $3.63\text{V}$. Connecting VDD to 5V will permanently burn out the sensor.

> [!TIP]
> **RULE 2: POWER THE MAX98357A AMPLIFIER FROM 5V, NOT 3.3V.**  
> - At 3.3V supply, the amplifier can deliver a maximum of $\approx 0.7\text{W}$ before heavy clipping and distortion occur.
> - At 5.0V supply, the amplifier cleanly delivers up to $3.2\text{W}$ into $4\Omega$ (or $1.8\text{W}$ into $8\Omega$).
> - The digital input pins (BCLK, LRC, DIN) on the MAX98357A are fully compatible with the ESP32-S3's 3.3V logic signals, even when VIN is powered from 5V.

> [!WARNING]
> **RULE 3: NEVER CONNECT SPK+ OR SPK- TO GROUND.**  
> The MAX98357A uses Bridge-Tied Load (BTL) architecture. Both output pins carry half of the amplified audio signal offset by DC bias. If either terminal touches Ground or VCC, the internal H-bridge MOSFETs will short-circuit and burn out instantly.

## 4. Preventing Audio Noise & Ground Loops

In battery- or USB-powered robotics, high-current switching loads (such as Wi-Fi radio bursts and display backlight PWM) can introduce audible "buzzing", "chirping", or "whining" into the speaker.

### Prevention Best Practices:
1. **Decoupling Capacitor on 5V Rail:**  
   Insert a **$100\,\mu\text{F}$ to $470\,\mu\text{F}$ electrolytic capacitor** (rated 10V or 16V) directly across the 5V and GND pins of the MAX98357A module on the breadboard. This absorbs sudden current draws during speech syllables and eliminates clicking sounds.
2. **Dedicated Power & Ground Return:**  
   Run the power wires for the MAX98357A amplifier directly back to the ESP32 5V and GND header pins rather than daisy-chaining through the sensitive INMP441 microphone power pins.
3. **Keep I²S Signal Wires Short:**  
   Keep jumper wires between the ESP32-S3 and the audio modules under $15\text{ cm}$ ($6\text{ inches}$) to minimize high-frequency clock radiation and radio-frequency interference.

---

## 5. Preventing USB Backfeeding

If you ever power the breadboard using an external 5V power supply while simultaneously connecting the ESP32-S3 to your PC with a USB cable for programming:
- **DO NOT** connect the external 5V supply and the USB 5V together directly.
- The ESP32-S3 DevKit contains an onboard Schottky diode (e.g. B5819W) between VBUS and the internal 5V rail to prevent current from flowing back into the PC's USB port. However, cheap clone boards occasionally omit this diode.
- **Recommended practice during testing:** Power the entire system directly through a good-quality USB data cable plugged into your PC or through the 5V, 2A USB power adapter connected to the ESP32-S3 USB port.
